Smooth, flat, and readable

Drywall is the invisible trade — when you do it right, nobody sees anything at all. Which makes the logo matter more, not less: the truck door and the bid header are the only places your work gets a face. The aesthetic should match the finish you sell — flat, clean, no ripples. A solid geometric badge with tight lettering says level-five finish before you say a word.

Avoid the temptation to draw the whole process. One knife, or one panel — not a mural of hanging, taping, and sanding crammed onto a door magnet nobody can parse at a stoplight.

Six drywall badges

Taping Knife is the wide blade every finisher runs a career on. Pan & Knife pairs it with the mud pan for a classic tools crest. Panel & Screws shows a sheet with its field screw pattern — hangers will clock it instantly. Corner Bead turns the humble bead profile into a sharp angular mark, Pole Sander flags the finishing side (and the dust everyone remembers), and Stacked Sheets reads supply-and-stock for crews that hang big footage fast.

Tap a badge and it opens in the full maker with your pick loaded — add the company name and go. HANG · TAPE · FINISH makes an honest tagline if you want a second line.

Finish with the paperwork

Grays were made for this trade — a slate or graphite main with one bright accent reads like fresh mud on new board. Run the one-color check before committing; drywall logos end up faxed on commercial jobs more than you’d think.

Then the sheets that pay: the bid by the square foot, the change order when the framer’s walls are out of plane and you’re floating them flat, the punch list of touch-ups after the painters. Send the logo to the free letterhead studio and all of it goes out branded.
