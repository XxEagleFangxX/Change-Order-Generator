Made to read through a dirty windshield

A mobile welder’s logo works the hardest in the trade — on a rig door caked in grinding dust, on a gate you fabricated, stenciled on a jobsite gang box. It has to read through grime and at speed. That means heavy, filled shapes with real weight, the visual equivalent of a solid fillet: no thin decorative linework that disappears the first time the truck gets dirty.

Steel trades earn trust through precision, and the logo should show it — one confident mark, square lettering, no flourish. The customer who hires you to weld a stair rail their kids will lean on is reading for exactly that.

Six welding badges

Weld Mask is the trade’s face — the auto-darkening hood every welder owns. Bead Seam stacks the dimes: a run of overlapping weld beads that any fabricator recognizes across a parking lot. Torch & Spark covers cutting and gas work, Spark Shower is pure grinder energy for shops that want motion in the mark, C-Clamp is the fitter’s badge — fit-up before fusion — and I-Beam Section flags structural work: stringers, headers, moment frames.

Stamp it on your paperwork

Charcoal and gunmetal mains with one hot accent — orange like fresh sparks or arc-blue — read welding immediately. Two colors also stencil cleanly, which matters when the logo ends up rattle-canned on equipment and gang boxes.

Fab work runs on paper more than people admit: the quote by the foot for handrail, the change order when the site measurements don’t match the drawings and material gets recut, the invoice with your certs noted. Push the badge to the free letterhead studio and it tops every sheet.

Questions

Free for a working welding business?

Yes — free to design, free to download, free to use on the rig and the paperwork. No account and no watermark. It’s one of the Digital Foreman Suite’s free tools.

Do I keep full ownership of the mark?

You do. The badge is drawn locally on your device and downloads straight to it — we never get a copy, so there’s nothing to claim, revoke, or relicense. The business name itself is trademark-office territory.

Can I get files for stencils and plasma-cut signs?

That’s exactly what vector is for. Free gets you the 1200×600 transparent PNG; Suite Pro adds a true SVG with outlined lettering plus one-color and reverse versions — the formats a plasma table or vinyl cutter actually wants.

One tap moves it to the free letterhead studio, and Suite Pro puts it on the live PDFs — change orders, bid comparisons, punch lists — across all three DFS apps.
