A trade built on straight lines

Tile setters live and die by layout — the dry run, the centered focal wall, grout joints that track true around three corners and meet themselves honest. A tile logo should look like it was set with spacers: geometric, even, deliberate. One strong pattern block or tool mark, name below, and it reads like a person who snaps chalk lines before spreading a single trowel of thinset.

The trap in this trade is prettiness — ornate mosaics and flourishes that vanish at truck-door distance. Save the artistry for the niche wall; the logo’s job is to be legible from the curb.

Six tile badges

Subway Offset is the running-bond wall everyone asks for — instantly readable as tile. Format Grid is its large-format cousin for crews slinging big porcelain panels. Notched Trowel shows the square-notch edge and the combed thinset ridges it leaves — a mark only trade people would design, which is exactly why it lands. Penny Rounds nods at mosaic and shower-floor work, Spacer Cross turns the humble spacer into a crisp emblem, and Diamond Emblem sets a tile on point for a classic badge feel.
