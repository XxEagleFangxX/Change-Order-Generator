One shape you can read from the curb

Your best advertising happens while the crew is still on the roof: the truck at the curb, the yard sign by the mailbox, the trailer full of tear-off. From that distance nobody reads a slogan — they read one shape. A clean ridge line or a gable silhouette says roofer before the homeowner across the street can even make out the phone number.

So the rule for a roofing logo is the same as for a ridge cap: simple, straight, and bold. One filled mark, a company name in tall lettering, maybe an established year. Anything more than that turns to mush on a door magnet — and mush doesn’t get the neighbor to walk over and ask for a quote on their own square count.

After the yard sign: the paperwork

Dark charcoal or slate for the mark reads as shingles; a red or orange accent reads as urgency, which storm work trades on. Keep it to two colors so it embroiders clean on caps and prints clean on door hangers.
