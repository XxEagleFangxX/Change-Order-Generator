Built one course at a time

Masonry already has the strongest visual language in construction — bond patterns, arches, joints struck clean. A mason’s logo should borrow from the wall, not from a clip-art folder. One solid mark that reads like laid units, your name set square underneath, and it looks like it was built, not decorated.

Remember where it lives: a truck door, a jobsite sign zip-tied to scaffold, the corner of an estimate. At those sizes, thin lines fill in and detail smears. A running bond block or a keystone silhouette stays crisp where a busy crest turns to noise.

The six masonry badges

Brick Courses is running bond with the head joints punched clean — the pattern every mason lays in their sleep. Trowel And Brick pairs the tool with the unit. Arch And Keystone is the showpiece: if your book is fireplaces, openings, and restoration, that’s the one. Chimney Stack flags chimney builds, rebuilds, and tuckpointing from the street. Stone Circle leans natural stone and veneer, and Block And Level is for CMU foundation and structural block crews who work to the string line every course.

Tap a badge and it loads in the full maker — add the company name and an EST. year, which suits masonry better than almost any other trade.

Questions

Is the logo mine to use anywhere?

Yes — trucks, signs, shirts, invoices, anywhere. It’s drawn entirely on your device and never uploaded, so no one else holds a copy or a claim. Protecting the business name legally is a trademark attorney’s job.

Free: a 1200×600 transparent PNG and a white-background version — sharp on paperwork and social pages. Suite Pro adds the vector SVG (outlined lettering, what sign and shirt shops request), hi-res exports, and one-color/reverse variants.
