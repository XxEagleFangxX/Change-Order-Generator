What reads on a framer’s truck door

Park at the lumber yard and look at the doors around you. The ones you remember have a single strong shape and a name you can read from across the lot — not a pile of hammers, houses, and phone numbers fighting for the same square foot. A framing logo has to work at string-line distance: on a door magnet doing 45 past the jobsite, on a yard sign at the curb while the sheathing goes up, at the top of a bid a GC skims in ten seconds.

That means one filled silhouette, heavy enough to survive a dusty tailgate, with your name in tall condensed lettering under it. Skip the thin-line clip art. A truss profile or a saw blade you can recognize at thirty feet beats six tiny tools you can only make out from the driveway.

Six badges cut for framing crews

Questions

Is the framing logo maker actually free?

Yes. Pick a badge, set your name and colors, download the PNG — no account, no watermark, no trial clock. It’s part of the Digital Foreman Suite, three free apps built for contractors.

Do I own the logo for my carpentry business?

Yes. It’s your name and your choices, drawn on your own device — nothing is uploaded and we keep no copy, so there’s nothing for anyone to claim back. Trademarking the business name itself is a question for a trademark attorney.

What files do I get, and what about vector for shirts?

Free gets you a sharp 1200×600 transparent PNG plus a white-background copy — right for bids, cards, and Facebook. Suite Pro adds a true vector SVG with the lettering outlined, which is what shirt printers and sign shops ask for, plus hi-res and one-color versions.

Can this go on my bids and change orders?

That’s the whole idea. One tap sends it to the free letterhead studio, and Suite Pro ($29.99 one time) puts it on every change order, bid leveling sheet, and punch list PDF the three DFS apps produce.
