Legible at a glance, like a panel schedule

Electricians sell trust in something invisible. The customer can’t see the difference between a tidy home run and a rat’s nest behind the drywall — but they can see your van, your shirt, and the header on your quote, and they judge the wiring by them. A clean, deliberate logo does the same job as a labeled panel schedule: it says this person is organized.

Every electrician gets tempted by the same three cliches at once — bolt, plug, bulb, all in one crest. Pick one. A single bolt in a solid badge reads from across the street; three ideas stacked together read like a garage-sale flyer.

The six electrical badges

Bolt Badge is the classic, done heavy and clean — the one that still looks right ten years from now. Breaker Panel is the deadfront every service upgrade revolves around; residential customers may not name it, but they’ve watched you work in it. Circuit Path traces a run with a node like a one-line diagram — sharp for low-voltage and controls work. Utility Pole suits line-side and rural outfits, Plug & Cord reads service-and-repair, and Bolt Bulb covers lighting-forward remodel work.

Tap one and it opens in the full maker with the badge set — add your name and license number line, then shuffle lettering until it looks like a company, not a template.

Wire it into your paperwork

Safety yellow on charcoal is the trade’s natural pairing — it’s the color language of arc-flash labels and it reads electrician instantly. Navy with a gold accent works if you want something calmer for commercial bids. Questions

Free as in actually free?

Yes — badge, name, colors, download, done. No account, no watermark on the PNG, no subscription. It’s part of the free Digital Foreman Suite for trade contractors.

Is the logo legally mine?

Yes. The design work happens entirely on your device and the file saves straight to it — we never receive or store a copy, so ownership isn’t in question. Registering your company name as a trademark is separate, and an attorney’s lane.

What file types, and can I get vector for the van wrap?

Free: 1200×600 transparent PNG plus white-background. Wrap shops want vector — Suite Pro adds an SVG with the lettering converted to outlines, hi-res PNGs, and one-color/reverse versions for stickers and stencils.

Can I use it on bids and inspection paperwork?

Yes — send it to the free letterhead studio in one tap. Suite Pro ($29.99 one time) prints it on the real change order, bid leveling, and punch list PDFs across all three apps.
