The first ninety-nine percent of a job builds the house. The last one percent builds your reputation. The final walkthrough is what your client remembers — and tells their neighbors about. Here is the punch list method that closes a job out clean, a worked example you can copy, and the free app that runs it with both signatures on the finish line.

Why the last week decides how the job gets remembered

By the end of a job, everybody's ready to be done — you, the crew, the client. That's exactly when little things slip: the door that needs one more adjustment, the touch-up in the hall, the cover plate that never got installed. None of it is big. All of it is what the client walks past every day after you're gone.

A good punch list turns that last week from a fade-out into a finish. Everything left gets written down, worked through, and checked off — and then you both sign that it's done. The job ends the way it started: everybody on the same page.

The punch list method, step by step

1. Walk the job with your client, not for them

Do the walkthrough together, room by room. When you're both looking at the same wall at the same time, you agree on what's left on the spot — and nothing new shows up a week later.

2. Write every item down, room by room

One item per line, tied to its room or area. "Touch-up paint — primary bath" is a different line than "touch-up paint — hallway." Specific lines get closed. Vague ones linger.

3. Tag each item by trade

Paint. Electrical. Plumbing. Carpentry. Flooring. Tags mean you can hand each sub exactly their items instead of the whole list — and nothing falls between trades.

4. Work the list, and check items off as they close

Close items in passes by trade. Checking each one off as it's finished shows your client real progress — a list that's visibly shrinking — and keeps the crew pointed at what's actually left.

5. Do the final walk, and get both signatures

Walk it one more time together, confirm every line is closed, and then you both sign. Now the end of the job is something you agreed on — in writing, with a handshake on top.

6. Hand over the signed completion PDF

The signed completion document is the natural moment for final payment. The job is provably done, both names are on it, and everybody leaves happy — which is where the next referral comes from.

Questions contractors ask about punch lists

What is a punch list in construction?

The list of remaining items — touch-ups, adjustments, small fixes — made near the end of a job, usually at a walkthrough with the client. The job is complete when every item is closed and both parties agree it's done. In some places it's called a snag list.

Who writes the punch list — the contractor or the client?

The best ones are written together, at a walkthrough with both of you in the room. Both sides agree on what's left while looking at the same work, and the list is complete the day it's made.

What should be on a punch list?

Every remaining item, one per line, tied to its room and tagged by trade. Specific, room-by-room lines get closed fast; vague ones linger.
